A consolidated view of what we've heard about our current messaging — from the site experience and content audit, internal stakeholder feedback, and Phillip R's April 9 stakeholder review.

Site experience & content · Internal audit (April 2026)
GapHomepage audit
Headline is too descriptive — it describes a category, it doesn't make a claim

"The global payments and software experts" describes a category. It does not make a claim. Any visitor who does not already know Flywire walks away with nothing memorable. The strongest commercial story we have never makes it to the hero.

GapHomepage audit
Leading with technology architecture, not client outcomes

"Next-gen platform, proprietary network, vertical-specific software" is architecture language. Buyers want to know what changes for their business, not how the engine is assembled. We describe ourselves when we should describe the world after Flywire arrives.

GapHomepage audit
The EDU gravity problem

A hospital CFO, a hospitality finance director, and a global B2B controller all land on the same page and have to work to find themselves. The visual and copy lean heavily toward education — our strongest vertical today, but a barrier for the other three.

GapHomepage audit
"Complex" as a selling point

Buyers do not want complexity. They want it gone. Celebrating complexity as a badge puts the translation burden on the reader rather than doing the work for them. The category win is turning complexity into control — not naming it.

OpportunityHomepage audit
The real story lives in the investor materials, not the homepage

Flywire is embedded infrastructure, not a standalone payment tool. That positioning exists in the investor materials but never makes it to the homepage where it would do the most work.

OpportunityHomepage audit
Integration names are buried

Ellucian, Epic, Workday, Oracle, Amadeus, NetSuite. These are the most powerful credibility signals on the page. They prove depth and switching cost without a single claim needing to be made. They are nowhere near the hero.

StrengthHomepage audit
What works: the four-vertical structure

Education, Travel, B2B, Healthcare as a navigation architecture is correct. It signals focus and depth. Keep it. Sharpen what sits inside each card.

StrengthHomepage audit
What works: the scale story

$37.6B moved, 240 countries, 140+ currencies, 5,000 clients. These numbers are real and they are large. They belong on the page early, framed as proof of embeddedness — not as investor metrics.

Within 30 days of going live, a Flywire client has measurable outcomes: seamless on-time payments from every market, fewer inbound inquiries, 100% of students with a payment plan who need one, error-free invoicing. That is a concrete, credible promise. It appears nowhere on the current site.

BriefThe frame this homepage should have been written from
"An end-to-end, embedded receivables platform. Not a standalone payment solution. Not a point software tool."

"The structural position: embedded in mission-critical financial workflows where data, compliance, workflow, and transaction execution intersect."

IR story · How corporate direction translates to the board/investor narrative
IRNarrative alignment
The commercial story and the IR story should be the same story — told in different languages

Today, the IR narrative ("embedded receivables platform · land and expand · operating leverage") is sharper than the commercial narrative. The opportunity in this workshop is to bring commercial messaging up to match, so both tell the same story with different vocabulary.

Commercial language
"The hardest payments problems are the ones we're built for." Speed to value in 30 days. Your team stops being the bridge.
IR language
"An end-to-end, embedded receivables platform." Sub-1% churn. 2–3× GPP expansion. 35%+ pipeline growth. Compounding operating leverage.
Shared proof
4,900+ enterprise clients. 8/8 Ivies. 64/71 AAU. Top-10 US health systems. 100+ certified integrations. $623M revenue, $13.5M GAAP net income.
Shared moat
Vertical software + integration depth + domain expertise + compliance posture. No entrant can replicate all four at once.

The alignment move: Give the IR moat language a home on the commercial site, framed as client benefit. Give the commercial speed-to-value promise a home in the IR narrative, framed as a leading indicator of land-and-expand velocity. Same story. Same proof. Different audiences.
